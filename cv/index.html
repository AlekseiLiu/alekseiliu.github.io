Research Highlights

  • Overview
    • My research spans two main areas: (1) generative AI for combinatorial optimization (neural architecture search using graph diffusion models steered by reinforcement learning), and (2) robust anomaly detection on dynamic graphs (from AutoML-based sparse models to temporal graph networks with probabilistic uncertainty modeling). The works address core challenges in automated model design, robustness, and interpretability.
  • DGPO: RL-Steered Graph Diffusion for Neural Architecture Generation
    • Technologies: DiGress (discrete graph diffusion), REINFORCE-style policy optimization, topological positional encoding, PyTorch, NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-201
    • Proposed DGPO, extending RL-steered discrete graph diffusion from undirected molecular graphs to directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) via topological node ordering and positional encoding. Enables controllable generation of neural architectures through reward-driven distribution steering.
    • Matches the NAS-Bench-201 optima on all three tasks (CIFAR-10: 91.61%, CIFAR-100: 73.49%, ImageNet-16-120: 46.77%; maximum validation accuracy by benchmark lookup, fixed cell connectivity so generation chooses operations). Achieves 94.50% on NAS-Bench-101 (benchmark lookup). Demonstrates transferable structural priors: pretrained on 7% of NAS-Bench-101 (topology generation), the best generated architecture lands within 0.32 pp of the full-data model and 7.27 pp above the pretraining ceiling (best generated architecture over evaluation draws, not a mean over all samples). 3 seeds; ~115 single-GPU hours on one A40. Multi-objective extension (MO-DGPO) reaches Pareto hypervolume 0.9901 on NAS-Bench-201, matching the union of three single-task runs (IJCNN 2026 paper).
    • Bidirectional-control ablation: inverse reward drives accuracy to 9.5% (chance), validating directional control of the generator.
    • Status: Accepted at IJCNN 2026 (WCCI)
    • Code: github.com/AlekseiLiu/DGPO
  • AutoML for Sparse and Interpretable Models
    • Technologies: TPOT, Optuna, NSGA-II, scikit-learn pipelines, multi-objective optimization
    • Developed iterative AutoML pipelines that explore the Pareto front between accuracy and sparsity, adapting architectures at different sparsity levels. Extended the approach beyond classification to outlier detection, enabling interpretable sparse anomaly detectors.
    • Demonstrated that different ML architectures are optimal at different sparsity levels for intrusion detection (UNSW-NB15, NSL-KDD) and outlier detection benchmarks (Cardio, Optdigits, Satimage-2). This positioned AutoML not as a black-box optimizer but as a tool for interpretable model design under trade-offs.
  • Dynamic Graph-Based Intrusion Detection
    • Technologies: Temporal Graph Networks (TGN), Deep Support Vector Data Description (Deep SVDD), PyTorch Geometric
    • Proposed TGN-SVDD, an end-to-end unsupervised intrusion detection model combining dynamic graph embeddings with deep one-class classification. Showed that vanilla TGNs, while strong for link prediction, are insufficient for intrusion detection, where anomaly detection objectives are critical.
    • TGN-SVDD: ROC-AUC 0.994 - 0.999 vs 0.268 - 0.690 for vanilla TGN on CIC-IDS2017 (ICANN 2023)
    • On CIC-IDS2017 data, TGN-SVDD outperformed classical anomaly detectors (LOF, Isolation Forest) and vanilla TGN, establishing dynamic graph modeling as a superior paradigm for network-based anomaly detection.
  • Robust Intrusion Detection under Noisy and Adversarial Conditions
    • Technologies: Probabilistic TGN-SVDD (RTGN-SVDD), Gaussian uncertainty modeling, negative sampling strategies
    • Extended TGN-SVDD into a probabilistic framework predicting Gaussian distributions over event embeddings. Introduced dual scoring mechanisms: one to filter noise, another to detect true attacks. Demonstrated robustness to increasing synthetic noise levels, where deterministic baselines failed.
    • RTGN-SVDD: ROC-AUC 0.574→0.844 and 0.561→0.880 under 50% label contamination vs the deterministic model (ESANN 2024)
    • Proposed a direction towards robust, uncertainty-aware graph-based intrusion detection, bridging the gap between academic benchmarks and deployment in adversarial environments.
  • Graph Learning by Dynamic Sampling (co-authored)
    • Technologies: Graph neural networks, dynamic sampling strategies
    • Co-authored work on dynamic sampling methods for graph learning, exploring how sampling strategies affect GNN training efficiency and performance.
    • Venue: IJCNN 2023
